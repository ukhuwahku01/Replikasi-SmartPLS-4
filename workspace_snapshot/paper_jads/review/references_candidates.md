## Literature rationale and defensible claim mapping

### Reliability, trust, and hypothetical acceptance

Trust in an automated system concerns an expectation of dependable performance under uncertainty; it should support appropriate reliance rather than unconditional acceptance [1], [2]. AI-specific trust literature also separates technological, contextual, and human influences [3]. Technology-trust work provides a conceptual reason to examine perceived reliability alongside trust [5], while technology-acceptance studies motivate examining trust and adoption intention without equating intention with actual uptake [6], [9], [10].

**Suggested manuscript wording:** “Perceived AI reliability may inform parents’ trust by shaping their expectations of the proposed system’s dependable functioning. Trust is particularly relevant where users cannot directly evaluate every inference made by an automated system. However, perceived reliability measured after exposure to a concept description is not an empirical measure of recognition accuracy or system dependability.”

**Do not infer:** that these sources validate KidSense’s actual AI accuracy, safety, suitability for children, parental acceptance in Indonesia, or future behaviour. Do not assert AIR is the same construct as trust, and do not assert adoption intention is observed adoption.

### Perceived explainability

The explainability literature provides a rationale for examining whether users believe that they can understand the reasons and information supporting an AI output, and how this relates to trust and acceptance [4]. Broader trust reviews support considering context rather than assuming explanations always improve trust [1]–[3].

**Suggested manuscript wording:** “Explanations may help users judge the basis and limitations of an AI recommendation. In the present study, Explainability refers to parents’ reported understanding of the explanations described in the KidSense concept, including reasons, supporting information, confidence and possible errors; it does not measure the technical fidelity of an explanation algorithm.”

**Do not infer:** that Shin’s scale was used, that KidSense provides validated XAI, or that a positive coefficient proves explanations caused greater trust. Actual item provenance must come from the researcher’s documentation, not retrospective citation matching.

### Perceived privacy protection

Information-privacy research shows that privacy-related expectations, perceived risks, and benefits are relevant to information-disclosure and technology-use decisions [7], [8]. This supports the practical relevance of parental perceptions about handling sensitive child-related information.

**Suggested manuscript wording:** “Perceived privacy protection concerns parents’ expectations that child-related information would be handled appropriately within the proposed design. Privacy-calculus and information-privacy research motivate examining such expectations in technology acceptance, while recognising that perceptions of protection are distinct from measured privacy risks, compliance, or verified security.”

**Construct distinction:** IUIPC [7] measures privacy concerns, not KidSense’s perceived privacy protection. Do not claim PP is reverse-coded IUIPC or an adapted IUIPC scale without evidence. Neither privacy paper by itself establishes a child-specific legal or ethical guarantee.

### Structural specification and exploratory M1

Trust/acceptance literature supplies a rationale for testing associations among perceived design attributes, trust and intention [1]–[10], but it does not retrospectively establish that all AIR/EXP/PP associations must operate exclusively through Trust. Direct associations with adoption intention can be considered as an explicitly exploratory alternative rather than used as proof that the original model was theoretically “wrong.”

**Suggested manuscript wording:** “Because the original model restricted the associations of the exogenous perceptions with adoption intention to an indirect route through Trust, an alternative model with additional direct paths was examined as a post hoc sensitivity analysis. Its results are interpreted as model-development evidence requiring theoretical justification and independent validation, rather than confirmatory support from a second study.”

**No fabricated hypotheses:** Unless the original pre-analysis record identifies prespecified hypotheses and scale sources, do not write that M1 hypotheses were preregistered, that direct effects were predicted a priori, or that the same-sample exploratory M1 is externally validated.

### PLS-SEM measurement and discriminant validity

Use [11] for general PLS-SEM reporting and [13] for conventional convergent-validity/reliability concepts. Use [12] for why discriminant validity requires explicit testing with HTMT rather than relying only on acceptable AVE/reliability or Fornell–Larcker results. Preserve the native software’s exact method labels: KidSense’s later native one-sided intervals are **BCa**, distinct from the earlier Python **BC** calculation. Do not attribute a BCa-vs-BC equality claim to [12].

**Suggested manuscript wording:** “Reliability and convergent-validity summaries were assessed alongside discriminant validity. HTMT estimates and the specified one-sided upper confidence limits were inspected against the prespecified 0.90 criterion; adequate reliability or AVE was not taken as evidence that all constructs were empirically distinct.”

**Finding boundary:** The earlier M0 native EXP–PP/EXP–AD upper limits above 0.90 must remain visible in the manuscript. If M1 becomes primary, report its own native HTMT values rather than reusing M0 figures or assuming added structural paths necessarily resolve conceptual overlap.

### Predictive assessment and CVPAT

Use [14], [15] to justify examining out-of-sample prediction separately from explanatory R². Use [16], [17] for testing predictive-loss differences rather than treating a few lower indicator RMSE values as proof of a statistically significant overall advantage. Positive Q²predict means better squared prediction loss than the IA benchmark; it does not establish superiority over LM. Negative CVPAT average-loss differences favour PLS, but the reported p-value governs the stated statistical test.

**Suggested manuscript wording:** “PLSpredict assessed held-out prediction errors against indicator-average and linear-model benchmarks, while CVPAT evaluated whether predictive-loss differences were statistically supported. Thus, positive Q²predict values and selected lower RMSE values were distinguished from an overall statistically significant improvement over the linear benchmark.”

**Scope boundary:** The actual native PLSpredict/CVPAT evidence currently documented belongs to M0. It must not be relabelled as an M1 prediction run. Direct-antecedent predictions for AD are conditional on observed TR; a different structural model can supply different predictor information. Comparisons require a matched information set and recorded folds/settings.

### Same-source, same-occasion survey limitations

Use [18] to motivate the limitation that self-report measures collected within the same survey can reflect common method influences. It does not show that such bias is absent or present in this dataset. Do not claim a statistical common-method-bias test was run, or that an unspecified diagnostic “eliminated” common method bias. It is appropriate to state this as a potential limitation and recommend temporal/method separation, objective use data, or independent validation in subsequent work.

## Citation-provenance guardrails

1. These sources support theory and methods. **None is evidence that the researcher adapted or translated its scales from that source.** The original questionnaire/source documentation is needed for scale-provenance claims.
2. Do not convert concept-exposure respondents into deployed-prototype users. Do not claim confirmed recognition performance, security, ethics approval or consent procedures solely from these references.
3. Do not cite M0 native prediction results as M1 evidence, and do not merge earlier Python BC and later native BCa intervals under an unqualified “bias-corrected” label where the distinction matters.
4. There is no arbitrary requirement to cite all 18. Prefer the articles actually supporting the manuscript’s claims; 14–18 is reasonable if each is cited substantively.
5. The list includes classic methodological papers and foundational acceptance work; their age is intentional. If the journal requires a recent-reference proportion, that requirement must be satisfied through a verified literature search rather than invented recent references.
